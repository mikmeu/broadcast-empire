# Decisions

What whoever works on Broadcast Empire needs to remember: how the game is put together where that differs from, or goes beyond, the design brief ([prompt.md](prompt.md)), and the facts that were expensive to find out. Numbers live in [src/config/balance.ts](src/config/balance.ts) and are not repeated here.

This is the short version, made for the first alpha (0.8.0) and kept up since. Until then every phase had its own numbered list (0.1 to 13g.6), which the roadmap in prompt.md still refers to. That full log is in the history: `git show phase-13g:Decisions.md`. What was open in it is now a request in [REQUESTS.md](REQUESTS.md).

## Settled by the designer (13 October 2026)

- **The application id is `media.mvdm.broadcastempire`,** for good.
- **The cheat console stays in the game,** in every build (Ctrl+Alt+C).
- **The CEO's office is for looks only,** and so is sitting next door to one's manager. Neither should change a number.
- **The dividend stays** as it is.
- **Research opens two ways:** with a second region, or with the biggest radio channel in one region.
- **Managers work in a management office,** a room apart from the staff, with a desk each.
- **The models built after the reference photos stay** as they are, cameras and lights at 45 degrees included. The master control desk keeps its photo look as tier 2 (Personal computer); before that it is a 1970s console.
- **The bail-out is €50,000, once** (see "Money and the market").
- **Penny's hint `places` stays:** once there is a second office she points out that people can be given a place of their own.
- **A TV sports show needs no rights.** Without them it talks about sport and draws less; rights for a season, or for an event while it is on, make it show the games.
- **A studio of its own kind for the talk show, the game show and the soap opera,** each built around its set, which stands nowhere else.
- **Television starts small:** Penny teaches a TV channel that only shows bought series, and a TV license is a fifth cheaper than it was.
- **Five shows first, then the schedule,** in Penny's first weeks.

On 14 October 2026:

- **The sounds and jingles stay** as they were picked (`SOUNDS` in `src/audio/sounds.ts`).

- **Shows are looked after on the schedule of their channel,** with the shows that are in no slot beside it; the On air window has Channels, Content and Ratings (and Streaming since 0.18.0, see 18 October).

## The simulation

- **`tick(state)` takes only the state.** The position of the random numbers is in the state, so the same save always plays the same.
- **Every player command is a pure action** (listed in `src/sim/actions.ts`, written by theme in `src/sim/commands/`) that returns a new state or an error with its reason. The interface, the test players and the cheat codes all go through them.
- **Balance and data are in `src/config/`,** never in the simulation or the interface.
- **Running costs build up daily and are paid on the 1st.** A month's result counts everything spent; the "running result" leaves out what was bought.
- **Much of what you see is for looking at only.** The clock beside the date, people's hours, journeys and meals, the traffic, the train, visitors and the audience: the simulation does not know about any of it. It still broadcasts the whole schedule every calendar day, and nobody works less for being at lunch. The town and who looks like whom follow from the game's seed and from names; nothing of it is saved. Everyone who comes here without working here has a made-up name (0.17.0, `day.ts`): the receptionist's from the seed, a guest's, a contestant's and the cast's from the show and their place, and the audience's from the show and the seat, counted across the blocks of a studio in the same order for the bus and the seats, so nobody changes looks on sitting down. Traffic has a state of its own that is not saved.
- **Writers in the hiring pool come from a stream of chance of their own (0.14.0)**, and so do the editors, and the audio engineers, cooks and clerks after them (0.15.0), and the recruiters and the people the recruiters bring in last of all (0.17.0); they take no number from the id counter (`generateCandidates` in `src/sim/generate.ts`), and a TV host's genres are drawn as before and a sitcom or reality TV is then left out. Everything else in a seeded game happens as it did before there were writers, so the test players' measurements still hold.
- **The region ids are the old names** (`capital`, `north`, `coast`, `heartland`, `highlands`) for Coaxia, Transponder Valley, Skywave Beach, Aerial Fields and Amplify Hills. Renaming them would break every save.
- **Cartoons on the market come from a stream of chance of their own (0.16.0)** (`CARTOON_STREAM` in `src/sim/content.ts`), so the other titles and the sports rights of a seeded game are what they were.
- **A day is 16 seconds at normal speed (0.16.0),** four times what it was, and the speeds stay 1×, 2× and 4× (the designer did not want 8×). Everything that goes by days slows with it, the office clock and the routine of the day among them; the town and its traffic run on real seconds and do not. The street's durations (how long ahead a bus or a taxi sets out, how long it stands, the walk from the stop) are written in hours of the clock the town was laid out by and converted (`townHours`, `TOWN_MS_PER_DAY`), so a bus still drives and stands as long in real seconds; only the timetable, a bus at a quarter to every hour, follows the slower clock, so buses come four times less often.
- **The control rooms and the master control room are in `src/sim/control.ts` (0.16.0),** with the cables: which channel goes out through master control (`throughMasterControl`: every channel with a license, since the designer's word after the build), which desks need a technician (`crewPosts`, master control first, then TV control rooms in the order of the building) and who works them (`crewing`: half a technician's day each, the longest hired first, the rest of their day for repairs). The pairing of TV studios and control rooms is in `src/sim/office.ts` (`tvControlRooms`), because the studio's kit needs it.
- **Made-up names are a prefix and a suffix from the designer's lists (0.17.0, `src/config/names.ts`, `src/sim/names.ts`),** written together as they are: people (a given name, then a last name from two parts), rival shows, rival companies and rival channels. Each name takes as many draws as the pick from a short list did before (a person two, a show or a company one), and a clash takes the next pair along instead of drawing again, so a seeded game plays out as it did and only the names differ. A rival channel's name is picked by a hash of its company, medium and number, not drawn. Games saved before keep the names they have; the lists carry no word filter on purpose.
- **A channel or show off the air is counted daily (0.16.0)** in `GameState.offAir` (`watchOffAir` in `src/sim/insight.ts`), and warned about once after a week; one that goes out again is forgotten. A show on a channel that is off the air is not counted apart: the channel's warning says it.

## Saves

- **A save is the whole state with a version number** (27 at version 0.10.0) and a step for every version before it, in `src/sim/save.ts`. Every change to the state needs a new step, and older saves must keep loading. Tests keep saves of older versions.
- **A step, once written, is frozen.** It may not call code that changes later; where it has to, it has its own copy (`firstFit`, `onOldPlan`).
- **A new field that holds a game day must be added to `DAY_FIELDS` in `src/sim/cheats.ts`,** or the `year` and `event` codes leave it behind. `event election` leaves `politics` where it is: the calendar goes to the election, not the election with the calendar.
- **Settings belong to the browser or the app, not to a save:** autosave, theme, reduce motion, volumes. The difficulty is fixed when a game starts and is in the save.
- **Saves have names; the autosave is one slot apart** and is written on the 1st of every month, and in the desktop app on quitting. The game opens on the home screen and loads nothing by itself. A save that cannot be read is listed with its problem and can only be deleted.
- **Browser saves and desktop saves are apart** (IndexedDB against JSON files), in the same format.
- **Older games keep what they had** whenever a rule changed: everything unlocked, hosts on two shows, lettered rooms as names, the standard building, pieces already standing in front of a door.
- **The rules of 0.16.0 give older games time, not exceptions (0.16.0):** a game on the air without a CEO's office or reception desk or without a master control room, or with a TV studio without its own control room, gets 30 days (`GameState.grace`, `inGrace` in `src/sim/office.ts`); after that the rule holds. The extra desks of a room that holds one are sold back at once, at their worth, the channel manager's kept first, because the designer asked for that.

## Money and the market

- **Radio and TV are separate markets,** each with its own leader. "Market leader" is more listeners across all regions than every rival company; "#1 in a region" is the biggest single channel there yesterday.
- **Licenses** are limited per region and grow with its people. TV licenses come on a fixed schedule. A license issued after the start is auctioned by sealed bid. Every license runs eight years and then has a renewal round, in which the holder has an advantage, the reserve is paid automatically, and a rival's license can be taken over. Giving one up bars that medium in that region for five years.
- **Rivals** have personalities and home regions, do not expand in 1970, improve a little every year, always use the equipment of their year, launch TV over time, and try to poach famous hosts at the going rate. The difficulty scales the quality of their shows (and, since 0.12.0, the player's costs: see below).
- **Dividends** are the money sink and a score (a pop-up to Finance on 1 January says what was taken, or, after Penny's December warning, why nothing was): each 1 January the owners take a share of the cash above a year of running costs. **Prestige**, from events the company stages and from awards, is a second score.
- **Radio and TV awards come every five years (0.18.0),** from 1975, so as not to crowd the player: nominations on 1 October, the night on 1 November (`src/sim/awards.ts`, `AWARDS`). Only a station's own productions are in the running; they are judged on the weekly reports on file, matched by channel and programme name, weeks off the air counting as none. The night draws on the main stream of chance. A winner of the player's asks for the going rate at their new fame (`Awards.raises`), and the player chooses: pay, or a drop in morale; nobody is raised without asking. "The show must go on" now asks for an event of the company's own (`Channel.lastEventDay`), since prestige alone no longer means one was staged. The night is a message and the Milestones window; a staged ceremony is a separate issue, to be released with this one.
- **The difficulty sets the money too (0.12.0):** the starting money, and one multiplier (`DIFFICULTY_COSTS`, applied through `costFactor` in `src/sim/difficulty.ts`) on rent, salaries, license prices and fees, and the prices of titles and sports rights. Building, furniture, episodes, events and marketing cost the same on every difficulty. Hard costs only 5% more: at 10% Penny's start no longer reached a month in the black outside the Capital.
- **Bills are paid every day (0.12.0),** a day's share of the month's salaries, rent and license fees, so the money in the status bar is what the company has; the 1st only brings the loan installment and the report. A save from before paid what had built up when it was opened.
- **A title is paid by the airing (0.12.0):** taking it from the market costs nothing, and each airing costs its price spread over its airings (`Title.perAiring`), as a running cost (`Ledger.titles`). Sports rights are still paid at once. A company in the red cannot take new titles.
- **Uncle Tabby sends money for milestones (0.12.0),** a sum per milestone in `config/milestones.ts`, scaled by the difficulty (`MILESTONES.rewardScale`). It is a gift in the books (`Ledger.gifts`): in the month's result, not in the running result. The dividend milestones bring nothing.
- **A sponsor's money is part of the ad revenue (0.13.0),** kept apart only for the reader: a day's result and the books have the sponsor's part (`sponsorRevenue`) and teleshopping fees (`fees`) as parts of `revenue`, not on top of it.
- **Music royalties are a share, not a price per hour (0.18.0):** each day a Top 40, rock or classical show pays `ROYALTIES.share` of what its slot earned, sponsor money included, times `costFactor`, as a running cost (`Ledger.royalties`, `payRoyalties`). A share can never make a music hour lose money, where a flat price would hit a small start hardest. The share was set low enough for the sensible test player's first January to stay in the black; since the records need a library (next entry) that January runs about €500 short and "Into the black" comes on 1 March. The morning show and talk pay none, nor TV: music videos pay for their music in their price. Record shelves (`DECOR.recordShelves`) are a decor piece in the radio studio, not the library; they are not on the studio's checklist, and the test players don't buy them. A payola scandal was left for an issue of its own: the game has no random scandals.
- **Music needs its records in the library (0.18.0, #43):** a Top 40, rock or classical show (`ROYALTIES.genres`) only goes on air when `GameState.records` holds its genre; without it `showQuality` gives a problem and the slot airs a rerun. A collection is bought once (`buyRecords`, `RECORDS.price`, paid as a purchase, not a running cost), never runs out, and takes `RECORDS.libraryUnits` places, one shelf, which `libraryUsed` counts with the titles. There is no starter collection: the first station builds a post room (a library needs one) and a library too, and Penny's three steps `music-post`, `music-library` and `music-records` come before the channel. The rule is on what is held, not on the room: a game from before (save 43) gets the records of the genres its shows play, free, and a library that is later sold does not silence a station. The records are bought on the Content screen, which opens for a company with a library as well as for one with a TV license; the market and the sports rights stay behind the TV license. The test players build their library and post room last, in the south row of the ground floor, so the north row keeps room for their canteen; tests that need that floor take the two rooms away with `withoutRecordRooms` and keep the records.
- **Campaigns are not a running cost,** though they are paid daily: they are chosen and can be stopped. Finance shows them under the running result with the total after them (0.12.1/0.13.0), and a campaign can repeat by itself.
- **Uncle Tabby bails the company out once.** At the month-end that would make it bankrupt he pays in a fixed sum, the bank starts counting again, and the next time is final. The sum is a gift: it is in no report and counts for no milestone. Penny has a line for it that can break into the first weeks, like the one about being in the red.
- **The calendar of the world is fixed, except for elections,** like the tech tree: World Cups, Olympics and recessions fall in set years, and nothing about them is random. Elections (0.18.0) are every four years unless the government falls, and the news announces a snap election two months before polling day; after it the four years count from it. Who is elected, whether the government falls and when are drawn on polling day from a stream of chance of their own, keyed on the seed and the day (`src/sim/politics.ts`), so a seed always has the same history and nothing else in the game moves. Everything that asks whether a campaign is on goes by `GameState.politics`, not by the date. The presidents and vice-presidents are for reading only (the wiki and the news); their names are never those of anyone else in the game.
- **Sponsor offers** come weekly from sales staff, are for radio or for TV, and stand on the best show that is still free. None come for radio or for TV while every show there has a sponsor.
- **Sports rights raise a TV sports show; they do not permit it.** Without any it draws 60% (`SPORTS_RIGHTS.talkOnly`); with rights for a season it draws by their prestige (80% to 125%), and during an event the company holds, in full. **Campaigns** need a free marketer.
- **Writers (0.14.0)** work at a writer's place in a writers' room that has its story board, and write two shows at most. A genre that is written says how many writers it needs (`writers` in `src/config/genres.ts`). On a show they lead, the sitcom, the soap and reality TV, there is no host: their writing counts where the host's skill would, with the same weight, and the show has no fame. On a show they only help, the best writing adds `WRITERS.bonus`. The cast of a show without a host is for looking at only.
- **Video editors (0.14.0)** work at an editing desk in an editing room, one to a produced TV show and two shows each, and the best editing adds `EDITORS.bonus`. The editing desk is on the TV equipment line. The research "Digital editing" still adds its 15% to production on every show: editors come on top.
- **A dressing room (0.14.0)** needs no staff: every make-up table in one that has all it needs makes up `DRESSING.hostsPerTable` hosts of TV shows, the most famous first (`madeUpHosts` in `src/sim/broadcast.ts`), and their shows gain `DRESSING.bonus`.
- **The talent programme (0.14.0)** is a course of a year for a weak host, without a stand-in: they have to be off every show first and can't be put on one meanwhile (`Employee.talentUntil`).
- **A host who takes a rival's offer works out a notice (0.14.0)** of `POACH.noticeDays`, presenting as before (`Employee.leaving`), and is courted by nobody else meanwhile. The offer can no longer be matched once it has run out, and letting a host go gives the same notice.
- **Recruiters (0.17.0)** are a job, not a department or a room: they sit at a workstation and belong to the support staff. Those at a desk bring `RECRUITING.extraEach` candidates a month each, at the rate cooks and clerks work (`helperRate`), the better of two skill draws (`bestOf` in `generatePerson`); only `RECRUITING.max` of them count. In 0.17.0 they could also arrange the talent programme; since 0.18.0 a talent manager in a workshop does that.
- **A recruiter's replacement for a leaving host is drawn from a seed of its own** (the host's id and the day, `findReplacement` in `src/sim/recruiting.ts`), not from the game's chance, so a game with a recruiter goes on as it would have. It is a candidate with `foundFor` set: it outlives the monthly refresh of the pool until it is hired or the host has gone (`removeEmployee` clears it), and `Employee.leaving.found` makes it once per host. An employee never carries `foundFor`.
- **A host presents one show a day;** production staff work on two at most. Everyone levels up, and a level brings a raise. The best producers on a show count, as many as its genre needs; each one beyond adds a little, so an extra one never hurts.
- **A course makes someone better at once, for money and a month of a weaker stand-in.** `Employee.trainingUntil` is the day they are back; while it is set, `skillAtWork` gives the stand-in's skill everywhere a skill is used. The head of the department has to be there, and one person per kind of job goes at a time.
- **A workshop gives courses in the house (0.18.0).** With a talent manager at the trainer's desk of a workshop that has all it needs (`workshops` in `src/sim/staff.ts`), they arrange every course instead of the head of the department, for `WORKSHOP.salaryMonths`, one to a training seat from any job. `Employee.courseIn` is the workshop someone sits their course in, so that the seats can be counted; a course outside has null there and is still one per kind of job, and stays open while every seat is taken. A course whose workshop is sold or whose talent manager leaves runs to its end. The talent manager also arranges the talent programme, which took a channel manager before; it takes no seat. The talent managers come last in the side stream of the hiring pool, so a seeded game draws everything else as before; the test players use neither courses nor the workshop.
- **A sponsor's rate goes by how well the company knows it** (`GameState.relations`, `RELATIONS`): 20% over the ad rate at level 1, 70% at level 6. The sales staff earn the points while a contract with that sponsor runs. Sales skill no longer sets the rate of an offer.
- **A staged show has its own kind of studio:** the talk show, game show, soap opera, sitcom, kids' and reality studios are each built around their set, which is on the room's checklist and can be placed in no other room; the reality studio also needs a confession booth. The news studio is for the news and for sports. Since 0.14.0 there is no production studio; a game from before turned it into the studio of the kind most made there, with that decor for free. A studio whose set has yet to be researched cannot be built until it is (`kindError` in `src/sim/rooms.ts`). Bought titles are scheduled as a "library program" that picks the title per day.
- **The Rooms window groups its tabs (0.18.0).** Every kind of room belongs to one group in `ROOM_GROUP` (`src/config/office.ts`), typed over `RoomType` so a new kind of room fails the type check until it has one. The window's top row is the groups, the second row the kinds in the open group; the open group comes from `catalogTab`, nothing is saved.
- **The streaming service (0.18.0)** is in `src/sim/streaming.ts`, its numbers in `STREAMING`. It opens with the research Streaming, two server racks in a library and ten shows or titles to offer. Its catalogue is the produced shows that went out yesterday (radio ones only after Podcasting) and the library's titles but teleshopping; a title on it costs one airing's price a month, so titles hoarded for it are not free. People who pay for streaming at all are a small share of the country, set so that a TV company that streams gets 10–25% of its revenue from it (measured with `streamBot`: 9% in 2012, 24–26% by 2029 on three seeds); they share themselves among the services by appeal (size × quality × freshness × price) against a baseline, and subscribers drift toward that, capped by the racks. The rivals' services, EPB+ (2014) and Nimbus (2017, from abroad), are fixed in config like the calendar of the world and have no state; the rival TV companies have none. Subscriptions are part of `revenue`, kept apart (`Ledger.subscriptions`) like the sponsors' part; the service's cost is a running cost (`Ledger.streaming`). It takes viewers from the player's own TV channels only (`watchedOnDemand` in broadcast.ts), so a game without a service plays as before.
- **Research is bought at once with research points.** Formats invented later are unlocked by research; rivals get them in their historical year. Equipment has tiers, and a studio is judged against the tier of its year.

## The office

- **Rooms are drawn.** A room is a rectangle of whole tiles (at least 3 by 3) with one door, and carries its own name, floor, place and door in the save. Open floor is corridor and costs nothing. A room costs a sum once per tile and rent per tile a month. Every room must stay within reach of the stairs and the lifts; the game refuses a room or a door that would cut one off. "Propose a place" puts a room of the usual size in the next free place along the hall.
- **Two buildings:** four floors in the towns, three longer floors in Amplify Hills and Aerial Fields (one more each since 0.14.0, opened by the company's value like the others). Stairs and lifts are at the front on every floor; the technicians' and cleaners' rooms are on the ground floor only. The reception comes with the building and is only written into a game once something is placed in it. Floors open with the company's value.
- **Furniture** is placed by hand, one piece at a time, with the pointer or with the arrow keys and Enter. A piece can be turned, moved for nothing and sold. It gives its whole price back for a week, then loses worth with the years and with wear. **The floor in front of a door stays clear,** from both sides: no piece there, and no door where a piece stands.
- **The first free spot is looked for from the wall across from the door.** The test players and the keyboard go by it. Filled from the door instead, an office loses workplaces and the balance tests fail.
- **A workstation is one piece** (desk, chair, machine), and wears and breaks as one. Managers only work at a manager's desk in a management office, writers at a writer's place in a writers' room and video editors at an editing desk in an editing room (0.14.0); a channel whose manager has no desk is off the air. The CEO has a kind of room and a desk of their own, one per company, which change no number, and never takes a manager's desk.
- **Who sits where:** someone can own a desk; owners are seated first and everyone else as before (newest desks first, production first, then by hiring date). While nobody owns a place a game plays exactly as it did. **Someone whose own desk is broken takes a free one,** since only desks that aren't broken are handed out (`workingDesks` in `src/sim/office.ts`).
- **The job buttons under a room's workplaces** give every place there that is nobody's own to one job at once (`seatGroup`), so that a department gets the office to itself.
- **Storage is a list, not a room (0.18.0):** `GameState.storage` holds furniture put away, exactly as it was (tier, condition, `boughtOn`). It is unlimited and free, takes no floor space, and its pieces don't wear (`wearingItems` only looks at rooms) but still lose worth with age. `companyValue` counts them at resale value, so storing never changes what a loan allows. A piece moved to another floor is carried there: changing floor no longer drops what is on the pointer (only the roof does), and `moving.roomId` is `null` for a piece that comes from storage.
- **"Go through them" and ‹ ›** step through a room's furniture one piece at a time, from the room panel.
- **Channel managers and department managers share the tab "Managers"** in the Staff window.
- **A show can be pressed and then put in a slot,** as well as dragged: the pressed show is shown beside the schedule and the next slot pressed gets it.
- **A control room** next door (a wall in common) lends its desk to a studio that has none, one desk per studio.
- **Upkeep:** rooms get dirty with use, equipment wears, the building has faults; cleaners, technicians and maintenance staff work down the worst first, without being assigned. Morale drifts toward a target set by dirt, faults, the canteen, comfort pieces and toilets, and cannot pass 100. Dirt and morale scale each person's own work.
- **Morale has room to grow (0.15.0).** The canteen gives half what it did, so that a cook, the post and meetings add something: what lifts everyone alike is `sharedMorale` in `src/sim/upkeep.ts`, and a team's meeting comes on top for its members. A cook and a post room clerk are desk workers whose desk is a stove in a canteen that has all it needs, or a sorting case in a post room (`DESKS` in `src/sim/office.ts`); their work scales by skill like the support staff's and by the room and their morale like a desk worker's.
- **Teams (0.15.0)** are a grouping of jobs in `src/sim/teams.ts`: the hosts, production (with writers, editors and audio engineers), sales, marketing, R&D and the support staff (cleaners, technicians, maintenance, cooks and clerks). Managers are in none. A team meets together and strikes together. Since 0.17.0 it also asks the office and the broadcast who meets with which channel; those imports are used only inside functions, so the cycle with `office.ts` and `broadcast.ts` is safe as long as nothing in them calls another module while it loads.
- **Someone can be promoted to head of their department (0.17.0)** (`promote` in `src/sim/commands/staff.ts`): production staff, writers, editors and audio engineers go to Production; sales, marketing and researchers to their own. They lose their shows, campaign and desk (`unassign` in `src/sim/staff.ts`, shared with firing), keep their skill, level and morale, and are paid at least what a manager of their skill is paid. The player is asked first, with what they leave named. A department has one head, and there is no way back down but firing. Hosts, channel managers and support staff cannot be promoted.
- **A channel manager leads the meetings of their channel's production team (0.17.0),** and the hosts have none. Who is in it is `channelCrews` in `src/sim/teams.ts`: everyone on the production team who is on a show of a channel whose manager is at a desk (its production staff, writers and editor, and the engineer `engineerRota` gives it), on two such channels the one with most of their shows, the first on a tie. It is worked out from the state each time, not saved, so it follows the schedule; it is cached on the state's arrays, because the broadcast asks it for everyone on every show. Those people sit in their channel's meeting (`GameState.channelMeetings`, by channel) instead of Production's, which is for the rest; strikes stay with the whole team. Team meetings get the rooms first, then the channels in their order. The engineers' rota leaves meetings out, so that who looks after a show does not hang on who is in a meeting.
- **A strike works by leaving people out (0.15.0):** a striker gets no desk (`employeesWithDesk`), so everything that counts desk workers counts them out; a host on strike is a problem of the show, so it goes to reruns; striking support staff are not among those who clean and repair; and strikers are not in the office (`peopleAt`). Nothing else checks for strikes. The warning is a day `warnedOn`, and the walk-out comes `STRIKES.strikeAfter` days later if morale is still low then.
- **A meeting is held at the start of a day** (`runMeetings` before the broadcast), so its lost work counts that same day: at half for the team's skill on shows, its department's power and an engineer's care. Its lift is fixed when it is held, by the seats of the room it gets (the largest free one) against the size of the team.
- **Audio engineers are not assigned to shows (0.15.0):** each looks after `ENGINEERS.showsEach` produced radio shows on the air, the best engineers the shows with the most listeners yesterday (`engineeredShows` in `src/sim/broadcast.ts`), as the dressing room picks its hosts.
- **Music schedulers are not assigned either (0.18.0):** each looks after `SCHEDULERS.channelsEach` radio channel that specialises in music (3 or more music slots), the best schedulers the stations with the most listeners yesterday (`scheduledChannels` in `src/sim/broadcast.ts`). The channel's music shows get `1 + SCHEDULERS.bonus × skill` on their appeal, on top of the specialization bonus; TV and other shows get nothing. If the music design (#2) brings playlists or formats, this is the part to redo; the job, room and desk stay. Nothing is saved: it is worked out each day. The schedulers in the hiring pool are drawn after the recruiters, before the people they bring in.
- **Guards are not assigned to hosts either (0.18.0, #41):** a host of fame `SECURITY.minFame` is a target for fans; each guard at a security desk covers `SECURITY.hostsEach` targets (× `helperRate`), the most famous first (`guardedHosts` in `src/sim/upkeep.ts`, as the dressing room picks its hosts). An unguarded target aims `SECURITY.penalty` lower in `moraleTarget`, and `runFans` gives each a `SECURITY.incidentChance` a day of a fan getting in, which costs `incidentMorale` at once. The chance is drawn from a stream of its own (`SECURITY_STREAM`, as `CARTOON_STREAM`) and only for unguarded targets, so a game with no host at fame 50 is exactly what it was. Nothing is saved. The guards come after the music schedulers in the side stream of the hiring pool, before the people the recruiters bring in, so a game without recruiters draws everything else as before. The news names the Staff window, as there is no window for the office to point at.
- **The roof is a view, not a floor (0.15.0):** the floor bar has a tab past the top floor, and the 3D office draws `Roof` instead of `Floor` there. The antenna and the dish are parts in `ROOF_ASSETS`, drawn from the state of the research; nothing is placed or saved.
- **A TV studio's control desk and audio mixer stand in its own control room (0.16.0),** and count in the studio's kit from there (`studioKit`); the studio's checklist still names them, so the room panel says where they go. A radio studio may still have its mixing desk in a control room next door (`borrowedDesks`), and gets 5% for it; a TV studio no longer does, since every one has a control room. A control room next to two TV studios belongs to the first in the order of the building that has none.
- **"Propose a place" puts a TV studio where its control room fits beside it (0.16.0),** and proposes a control room beside a TV studio on the floor that has none; a floor without such a place has no proposal for a TV studio.
- **One desk to a room is a rule of placing (0.16.0)** (`ONE_DESK_ROOMS`, checked in `fitError`), for the management office, the editing room and the audio room. The designer's "etc." was read as those three; the post room and the writers' room hold as many as fit. Their usual size is 3 by 5, the audio room's stays 4 by 5 for the recording booth.
- **A post room comes before the library (0.17.0, #42):** without a post room that has its sorting case (`isFurnished`; no clerk needed), a library can't be built or made from another room (`kindError`, which the build menu and the room drawing show) and no title can be bought (`buyTitle`); `postRoomNeeded` in `src/sim/content.ts` holds the rule. The first server rack in any library lifts it, because titles that come over the wire need no post; it is checked live, so selling the last rack, or emptying or closing the post room, brings it back. A library that stands keeps airing what it holds. A game from before with a library and neither gets 30 days (`grace.postRoom`), as the rules of 0.16.0 did. Sports rights are not titles and need no post room.
- **Decor that does something has its numbers in `DECOR` (0.16.0),** and one piece of a kind counts per room: the calendar and the sales chart lift the effective skill of production and sales staff whose desk is in that office, the printer takes 10% off what every campaign costs (its strength still goes by its budget), the bin and the dishwasher slow the dirt of their room, a flip chart makes the meetings of its room lift more, and an aquarium is three comfort pieces in one (`comfort` can be a number).
- **The cables are laid by themselves and only shown (0.16.0):** the cable view draws them from the doors of the rooms along the corridor to a shaft in the hall by the stairs, colored by whether they carry a channel on the air, and lists them. Laying cables by hand was left out: it would be a second way to lose a channel that the master control room already is.
- **People find their own way** over a grid per floor: through the middle of a doorway, never through a wall, around furniture where there is room and through it only when there is none. People do walk through each other.
- **The traffic keeps vehicles apart by its rules, not by luck** (`src/office/traffic.ts`), and a test drives every town, also at four times the frame length, looking for two that touch. Each lane is a queue; a vehicle never comes closer to the one in front than a gap. A crossing is taken only on green and with room to stand clear beyond it, and its lights go to the other street only once it is empty. A right turn crosses no other lane, so it needs no more than its own street's green and room in the street it turns into. Since 0.11.0:
  - **A turning vehicle stays in its old lane's queue until its tail is round the corner, and whoever follows it waits at the start of the bend:** on the way round its tail swings across the lane, which a follower that only saw the middle drove into.
  - **Oncoming traffic stops short of where an overtaker ends,** and the overtaker only sets out when everyone coming can still stop. Asking for an empty lane instead (long enough for the fastest vehicle) meant nobody ever overtook in these short blocks. The overtaker stops far enough behind the standing bus or taxi to be over in the other lane, tail and all, before it is level with it.
  - **A bus or a taxi that is due, and whoever is in its way, get green sooner and keep it while they are about to go,** and reckon with every set of lights on their way. Without that a bus in Transponder Valley, where half the traffic is trucks, was late about one time in a hundred. Nothing comes into town by the bus's road just before the bus, and nobody turns in ahead of it.
  - **In a town by the water the side streets end at the street along the shore:** whoever comes down one turns there, nobody turns into one there, and nobody comes up one from the water.

## The player's way in

- **A new company starts with what it takes to make radio.** Six things open later, each with a milestone (`src/config/unlocks.ts`): more regions, sales, marketing, research, the content market, events. A locked window stays in the menu, dimmed, and says what opens it. Older tests of a feature start from `unlockAll(...)`; the tests that play decades start locked.
- **Penny teaches one opening, tested to work:** a CEO's office and a reception desk, a radio studio, a master control room, an office, a management office, a channel manager, five hosts, three producers, a cleaner, a technician, a license in Coaxia and five shows; then sales, a first sponsor, marketing and a first campaign. It ends a month in the black by March on every difficulty and from every headquarters, except for one start in four tested on hard outside Coaxia since 0.16.0 (the master control room and its technician). The first hiring pool of a new game has a host for each of those five shows (`OPENING_SHOWS`). Smaller openings all lose money, and so does a single license outside Coaxia, which is why she sends everyone to Coaxia first.
- **Penny starts with a look around and the company itself (0.16.0):** a talk step on turning, zooming and moving the view (the view is not in the game state, so it is done when answered), then the CEO's office and the reception desk, before the studio; nothing goes on the air without them. The master control room follows the studio's equipment and a technician the cleaner; she says the cheapest technician will do, since skill matters little at the desk. Her line for the studio begins “Now the heart of it”, as it no longer comes first (0.17.0). She explains the office clock in a step of its own after “On the air”, her television tour has no master control step, the room being there from the first weeks; her line for the TV manager gives them “an office of their own” and names no room, since a manager needs a management office of their own and the TV manager's is a second one (0.17.0).
- **"In the black" means the running result,** for Penny and for the milestone "First profitable month" as on the monthly report: income less salaries, rent, license fees and the like, without what was bought once. A January that pays its way finishes her step on 1 February and opens Sales that day.
- **Penny has a second chapter, television** (`TV_STEPS`): it starts when the player says yes to her hint `television`, runs as steps with a ring like the first weeks, and is kept apart from them. She offers it once the company is steady (`TV_OFFER`: six monthly reports, the last three in the black by the running result) and has the money, not after its first good month (#50). "Not now" is remembered as `television-later` in what she has said; "Walk me through television" in the Settings starts the chapter again whenever the player wants it, also after it was done, and calls her back in. Steps the game counts as done are skipped, and a TV channel on the air leaves only her last word. A player who sent her away during the first weeks counts as past them. What it teaches is one program of bought series: measured, that pays its way after a few months and a program of films does not.
- **A line that waits for its recording is not played** (`TO_RECORD` in `src/config/voiceOver.ts`), so an old recording never says other words than are on screen. A test holds the list against the folder.
- **A step is done when the game says so,** not when a button in the tutorial is pressed. Hints come after the first weeks, at most one a week unless something has a deadline. Penny never points at a lock. During the chapter on television, the urgent hints that the TV channel itself sets off (`TV_WAITS`: a license or rights auction, titles running low) wait until it is done; the others still cut in, and the chapter always carries on at the same step afterwards, which her window says under the hint (#50).
- **Every line of the tutorial is in `src/config/guide.ts`.** What is spoken names no player, no company and no figure, so a recording stays right. `design/voice-over.md` is written from it by `npm run voice-over`, and a test fails when it is out of date. **All 55 lines are recorded (0.10.0) and must not change without asking;** `first-month` and `black` got new words in 0.10.0, at the designer's word, and were recorded again with `bailout`. The recordings are the designer's, in `public/voice/`, one mp3 per line named after it. A line is said once, when it comes up in a game (`src/audio/voice.ts`); one without a recording is shown and not heard. The voice has its own volume.
- **The wiki's figures are the game's own,** filled in from `config/` and `sim/`. A part that depends on the game that is open (the recessions so far, how well you know a sponsor) is a block written from that game and its day (`asOf`); before a game is open, and for searching, it reads as at the start. Each sponsor in `SPONSOR_NAMES` has a page (`SPONSOR_ARTICLES`), with its logo in the box of facts; a test holds the two lists together, so a new sponsor needs a page. A test reads Penny's lines and the whole wiki for words that are gone (lettered rooms, renting a room, removed buttons).
- **A region's article has the same parts in the same order** (0.18.0): the lead, The people, Sport, The capital, What to see, For a broadcaster; a test holds it. Its teams, capital and seats in the senate are in `src/wiki/lore.ts`, written as of 1970. An article may have a picture at the top of its box of facts, a file in `public/wiki/`; for a region it is a screenshot of its town, until drawn landmarks come with #24.

## Interface

- **The office is always on screen;** everything else is a window over it that can be moved, and several can be open. The status bar is part of the page.
- **Windows can be resized as well as moved** (0.17.0), by their right edge, bottom edge and the corner between them; not the left or top, so the title bar and the menu are never grabbed by mistake. A double-click on the corner puts the normal size back. The size, like the place, lasts until the game is closed: neither is saved. Penny's window and the cheat console keep their size, and on a small screen a window still takes the whole stage. **The layout inside a window follows the window's width, not the screen's:** `.window-body` is a container named `window`, and a layout in a screen narrows with `@container window (…)`, never `@media`.
- **Every color is a token** at the top of `src/ui/styles.css`; channel logos and sponsor logos are the only colors outside them. The game writes the theme on the page itself (`data-theme`); the stylesheet does not ask the system. The 3D view reads the same tokens.
- **By the time of day (0.15.0, the default),** the scheme follows the office clock of the game that is open: dark before sunrise and after sunset, by month (`DAYLIGHT` in `src/ui/theme.ts`), and light with no game open. The office day is a month long, so the scheme turns twice a month at most. A stored “system” setting reads as the time of day.
- **A new game opens in the light (0.16.0):** January's sunrise on the office clock is at 8, when the office day starts, so the first screen a player sees is not dark. The clock in the bar and the color scheme in Settings say how the clock goes.
- **The 3D libraries load only when the office opens.** Reduce motion stops walking, traffic and the ticker.
- **The view turns in quarter turns** and is moved with the keys or by dragging it: the mouse or one finger moves it, two fingers move and zoom. A press that travels less than a few pixels stays a click, and the click that ends a drag is swallowed. While a room is being drawn the left button and one finger draw, so the view goes with the right or middle button or two fingers.
- **News the player will want to act on names its window** (`about` on a message): it gets a pop-up with a button to that window and a dot on its icon. Sponsor offers, an ended campaign, something opening, a rival's offer to a host, a license running out and its renewal round, and a technology coming within reach do this. Dots are not saved; `waitingFor` gives a loaded game the ones for what is still waiting.
- **Every message is an e-mail in the inbox (0.17.0),** from the person it concerns, picked when it is sent and saved as a copy (`from`), so it reads the same after they leave. `addMessage` takes the desk as its last argument; without one, `deskOf` in `src/sim/inbox.ts` takes it from the topic and window. A channel's own news comes from its own manager (`fromChannel`). Milestones, the dividend, the red and the bailout are Uncle Tabby's, written in his voice; the market (events, rivals) and any desk with nobody at it are Penny's. Priority is worked out from tone, topic and window (`priorityOf`), never saved; low-priority news gets no toast and only lands in the inbox. A new message needs a desk if its topic and window don't say who sends it; the inbox test checks every desk has someone in a fully staffed company.
- **Every control gets its sound from one listener on the page,** by what kind of control it is; `data-sound` overrides it. A failed action sounds different. One jingle at a time, for the message that is shown. A test fails when a clip is listed but missing, or present but unused.
- **What the schedule tells the player is worked out apart from the simulation (0.13.0),** in `src/sim/insight.ts`: who is tuned in to a slot and how well a genre suits them (fit counted by people and what advertisers pay for them), specialization, how long bought titles last and the freshness they settle at, and why a slot's audience moved. `slotFactors` in broadcast.ts takes a slot's appeal apart with the same parts the share formula multiplies; each Monday they are kept as `weekAgo` for the arrows and the news of the next week.
- **The game asks for a newer version only in the desktop app (0.13.0),** through its main process (the page itself may load nothing from outside), once a session, and only when the setting is on. It reads the latest GitHub release and that version's CHANGELOG.md.
- **On air is organised by channel (0.11.0).** Its menu has Channels, Content and Ratings, Staffing (0.17.0) and, since 0.18.0, Streaming, at the designer's word, locked until Streaming is researched; a channel's schedule is a page of Channels (`src/ui/onAir.ts` holds which), with the shows of its medium and the buttons that make them. A show is looked at, changed, revamped and cancelled there.
- **Staffing (0.17.0) is a report, not a second place to change shows.** The section of On air shows each studio's day, every produced show with its crew, and who has room for another show; its Change button opens the show's form on its channel's schedule (`edit` in `src/ui/onAir.ts`). It counts producers by `producersAtWork` in broadcast.ts, the same helper the ratings use, so a show is "short" exactly when its production drops: fewer producers at a desk than the genre's `requiredStaff`. A strike keeps everyone home and has news of its own, so it never makes a show short. The game keeps which shows on the air were short (`shortOfProducers`) to tell the player once when one falls short, as `offAir` does for a week off the air.
- **The Staff window sorts and filters its list in `src/ui/staffList.ts` (0.17.0),** apart from the window so that it can be tested. The choices last until the game is closed and are not saved. The "Needs attention" toggle uses `needsAttention`, the rule behind the dots on the tabs, and "Low morale" uses `STRIKES.below`. An audio engineer's shows are those `engineeredShows` gives them, as their tag counts them. The controls show only with more than `CONTROLS_FROM` people, and count only while they show.
- **A milestone pauses the game** with a pop-up of its own, which also names what it opened; those openings get no small pop-up besides. The store notices both by comparing the game before and after a day or an action (`announce` in `src/ui/store.ts`).
- **Rooms have no buttons above the office (0.11.0).** A room's name label in the office can be clicked, and carries the `data-guide` that Penny's ring looks for; the Office window lists the rooms for the keyboard.
- **Penny's ring finds its control by `data-guide`.** What she points at has to stay in sight, which is why some buttons sit in a sticky footer; a control that is scrolled out of sight in its window is brought into view, a few times and no more. Her "Show me" can also clear the view (`floor`) for a step that starts in the office.
- **The story figures gesture by code, not by clip:** their files have no talking movement, so `StoryFigure.tsx` turns the arm and head bones on top of the idle clip while their own line is heard, and hangs a mouth on the head that follows the loudness of the voice (`voiceLevel`).
- **Models** are Kenney's kits (furniture, characters, three city kits, the train) and Quaternius's two figures, all CC0, in `public/models`. Broadcast equipment and vehicles are built from simple shapes in the registry, `src/office/assets.ts`, where every piece is listed part by part. Characters are recolored by pointing a part at another swatch of the kit's one texture. The black-haired ones have their eyes and mouth painted from the hair's swatch, so a new hair color leaves alone the small pieces of that swatch (`FACE_PIECE_POINTS` in `office/looks.ts`); a beard is a bigger piece and takes the hair's color.

## The desktop app

- **Electron,** not Tauri: the same engine on every system for the 3D office, and nothing to install beyond Node. Only `electron/` and the `build` block in `package.json` depend on that choice; the game talks to the desktop through `src/persistence/desktop.ts`.
- **The page runs in a sandbox** and can ask the app for save files and closing, nothing else. Only what ships with the app is loaded. One copy runs at a time.
- **Nothing is signed.** The installers are built on a Mac: one for every Mac (Apple silicon and Intel in one), 64-bit Windows and 64-bit Linux.
- **The version** is in `package.json` and reaches the game through `src/version.ts`.

## Tests and test players

- **The test players in `src/sim/bots.ts` play with the actions a player has:** they build where the game proposes a place and furnish piece by piece. Short cuts for tests only (lettered rooms of the old plan, furnishing in one go) are in `src/sim/fixtures.ts`, which the game does not use.
- **The test players build their CEO's office at the far end of the ground floor (0.16.0),** after their first rooms, so that the rooms they build from the stairs stand where they stood before; they hand the game a state without their own `lastId`. The test players build a master control room with their first station, at the far end of the north row, and hire a technician for it; tests that build channels by hand set one up with `setUpMasterControl`; the test shortcut `autoPlaceFurniture` puts a desk that a one-desk room has no place for in a new room of that kind, and `addMissingEquipment` builds a TV studio its control room.
- **The balance is guarded by tests that play years:** a first profit from every region and difficulty, ten-year survival, a 1970–2030 run, license renewals, the edge that research gives (an average over three games, because luck in the hiring pool moves it by a few percent).
- **What those tests have shown:** a company that never hires support staff is bankrupt within two years; one that stays in one region and never researches does not last ten; one well-run channel in Coaxia earns a little on normal and fails on hard. Since 0.12.0 `npm run simulate` has the bots, and the TV path Penny teaches was measured by a player who follows her to the letter: on normal it breaks even in its first months and then earns a few thousand a month; series at lunch lose about two thirds of what they cost.
- **What 0.16.0 did to the measurements:** research gives a smaller edge (about 1.7% more listeners over twenty years, against 3.5%, and in one game of three it comes out even): every manager has a room of their own, and that floor is floor the company that researches more would have grown on; the guard is now 1% on average, and no game more than half a percent short. A second station aimed at other audiences draws more than a second one like the first over three games together, but not in each (seed 1 draws a few per cent less); the guard compares the three together. A company that neglects its support staff still draws a third less after half a year (it was more than 40% less). With a master control room and its technician needed from the start, the growing test player goes bankrupt on hard in two games of six (none in 0.15.0); on normal and easy none does.
- **The advice in the wiki is tested where it can be (0.13.0):** a test player that aims its second station at other audiences (`networkBot`) has to draw more listeners than one whose second station plays the same music (`growthBot`).
- **The whole suite has to run alone** and takes about two and a half minutes. Tests that play years carry their own time limit. If the Mac sleeps during a run, tests time out for no fault of the code.
