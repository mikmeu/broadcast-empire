## History

The Republic of Etheria is a federal republic located in the heart of the Union. Since 1769, the country has consisted of five semi-autonomous regions: Coaxia, Amplify Hills, Skywave Beach, Transponder Valley, and Aerial Fields. Today, Etheria is known for its strong economy, extensive transportation network, questionable corporate influence, and the national belief that every issue can be solved by forming a committee.

Before unification, the territory that now forms Etheria was divided among dozens of kingdoms, duchies, counties, principalities, and one short-lived republic that reportedly collapsed after its government spent six months debating the design of its flag.

Throughout the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, the region was plagued by border disputes, trade conflicts, and the occasional war over topics historians now classify as "surprisingly minor." While the coastal states of Coaxia and Skywave prospered through trade, the interior territories often struggled to cooperate with one another.

The modern republic emerged following the Congress of Turing in 1769. Facing economic stagnation and growing pressure from neighboring powers, the various rulers agreed to dissolve their independent states and form a single federal republic. The arrangement was intended to create stability, encourage trade, and reduce the number of customs checkpoints a traveler would encounter during a single afternoon.

The unification was widely considered a success, although several former nobles continued referring to themselves as kings and dukes for decades afterward. Some descendants reportedly still do.

The five regions retained significant autonomy, creating a political system in which most decisions could be debated at both a regional and national level. Supporters describe this as a healthy federal balance. Critics describe it as a reliable way to turn a two-week project into a two-year project.

### Geography and Neighbors

Etheria shares borders with three neighboring countries.

To the west lies the Kingdom of Casette, which borders Skywave Beach, Amplify Hills, and Coaxia. Casette is one of the oldest monarchies in the Union and is known for its long political traditions, rainy weather, and ability to remain remarkably calm while its neighbors reorganize themselves every few decades.

To the south, between Coaxia and Aerial Fields, sits the Duchy of Sants-de-Inez. Despite its small size, the duchy enjoys one of the highest incomes in the Union and serves as a major center for banking and finance. It is also famous for its low fuel taxes, leading many Etherians to make suspiciously frequent "cultural visits" across the border.

To the east lies the Federal Republic of Dramedy, Etheria's largest neighbor and trading partner. Dramedy is renowned for its engineering, manufacturing, and administrative efficiency. Its citizens have a reputation for planning everything in advance, which continues to confuse Etherians who prefer solving problems after they occur.

### The Modern Republic

The nineteenth century saw rapid industrialization, transforming Etheria into one of the Union's leading economies. Railways, factories, ports, and later airports connected regions that had once spent centuries treating one another as foreign countries.

During the twentieth century, the country experienced substantial economic growth, the rise of powerful national corporations, and the emergence of mass media. It also developed a political tradition in which every major scandal eventually produces an investigative committee, followed by a reform package, followed by a second scandal.

Today, Etheria is widely regarded as one of the most prosperous and influential republics in the Union. Its citizens enjoy a high standard of living, strong public services, and a remarkable ability to complain about both simultaneously.

## Geography

Etheria lies on the sea, which runs along its whole north coast and cuts in as a long bay on the east side. To the west and south it borders other countries. A river runs from the Amplify Hills south-west into Byte Lake, and another runs down the east of Transponder Valley to the bay.

## Politics

Every region has their own Governor and have their own house of state representatives. The number will vary per region. Nation-wide the country has a President and a Vice-President who choose their own ministers. The country has both a senate (formed by a couple of senators per region) and a directly elected house of representatives.

Regional senate elections are every four years, just as the elections of the house, but they are two years apart. The leader of the biggest party in the house will become the newly elected President, the leader of the second biggest party the newly elected Vice-President. The leader of the biggest party in the Senate will be the Senate Speaker.

The senate consists of 136 seats, made up of 50 by Coaxia, 36 of Transponder, 24 of Skywave, 16 of Aerial and 10 of Amplify.

The house consists of 185 seats. In order for a party to be part of the house the party needs at least 250.000 votes. The remainder of the seats will be divided by the remainder of the parties. Major parties include: Etheria Workers Force (EWF), Liberath'49 (LIB), The Green Society (TGS), Righteous Etheria Brothers (REB) and Free Etheria (FE).

## Sponsors

### Beerstar

Beerstar is an Etherian beverage manufacturer and one of the country's oldest companies. Founded in 1607, it is best known for producing the popular Beerstar line of beer-adjacent beverages. The company is headquartered in Brassport and sources much of its agricultural supply from the fertile Amplify Holls region.

Although commonly referred to as a brewery, Beerstar has spent much of its modern history involved in legal disputes concerning whether its flagship product legally qualifies as beer. The company itself avoids the discussion, preferring to describe Beerstar as "a refreshing lifestyle beverage."

"I always say: we don't just make a drink, we make an experience. Also, by law, we're not allowed to call it beer."

— Zinky Bulk, CEO of Beerstar

The company's main products include Beerstar Regular, Beerstar Light, and Beerstar German, a cola-and-sinas mixture whose classification remains disputed by beverage experts. Seasonal editions include Beerstar Winter, Beerstar Tropical, and Beerstar Limited Collector's Barrel Reserve Premium Gold Signature Edition.

Beerstar is one of the largest sponsors of sports, music festivals, and public celebrations across Etheria. Critics have noted that many of these events appear to exist primarily to advertise Beerstar products.

The company slogan, "Taste History", was introduced in 1984 and remains unchanged despite several attempts by historians to distance themselves from the brand.

### Citrus

Citrus is a beverage company headquartered in Coaxia City and the market leader in soft drinks throughout Etheria. Its flagship product, Citrus Fresh, is among the most widely consumed beverages in the country.

Originally founded as a lemon processing company, Citrus expanded into consumer beverages during the 20th century and quickly became known for its aggressive marketing campaigns, bright yellow branding, and remarkable ability to add sugar to nearly any product.

"We're the creators of the sweetest and sourest drink in the world. And it's definitely made from real lemons."

— Samantha Sour, CEO of Citrus

In addition to Citrus Fresh, the company produces Citrus Max, Citrus Zero, Citrus Ultra, Citrus Extra Ultra, and Citrus Kidz, which contains approximately three times the recommended daily amount of excitement.

The company has received both advertising awards and public health criticism. While Citrus products are enjoyed by millions of Etherians each day, nutrition experts frequently identify the company as a significant contributor to national obesity statistics. Citrus disputes these claims and encourages consumers to "exercise their freedom of choice."

Citrus spends more on advertising than any other beverage company in Etheria. Market analysts estimate that the average citizen sees a Citrus advertisement twelve times before breakfast.

### Ipsum & Ipsum

Ipsum & Ipsum is one of Etheria's largest law firms, specializing in litigation, corporate law, regulatory disputes, intellectual property, and situations that have become much worse than expected.

The firm was founded in Coaxia City by siblings Lareem Ipsum and Dolores Ipsum. It rose to prominence during the 1990s through a series of high-profile court victories involving major corporations, government agencies, celebrities, criminals, and, on at least one occasion, an escaped zoo animal.

"If there's a lawsuit somewhere, we want to win it. Whether it's murder, tax evasion, or someone claiming their neighbour stole a garden gnome. Ipsum & Ipsum is the choice for you."

— Lareem Ipsum, Co-Founder

The firm's most famous case remains The State of Etheria v. Beerstar, in which the government attempted to force Beerstar to remove the word "Beer" from its name. The proceedings lasted nearly three years and generated more media coverage than several national elections.

The case ended in a confidential settlement. While the details remain unknown, legal scholars generally agree that the only clear winner was Ipsum & Ipsum's billing department.

The firm's headquarters occupy an entire city block and contain more lawyers per square meter than any other building in Etheria.

### Tahoma

Tahoma is an international fashion retailer known for producing affordable clothing basics for the mass market. The company operates hundreds of stores across Etheria and exports products to dozens of countries.

Its manufacturing facilities are primarily located in the industrial region of Transponder Valley, where millions of identical T-shirts are produced each year.

"At Tahoma, being your best normal self is your best self."

— Adam Blend, CEO of Tahoma

Tahoma's product lineup includes plain shirts, plain jeans, plain jackets, and seasonal variations of those products that experts describe as "almost exactly the same."

The brand is famous for embracing normality as a marketing strategy. While competitors chase fashion trends, Tahoma prides itself on producing clothing that nobody will ever comment on.

The company received international recognition after winning the prestigious Dullest Brand on Earth award six consecutive years. Tahoma's marketing department reportedly celebrated the achievement with a remarkably average office party.

### Foodst

Foodst is Etheria's largest restaurant operator, managing over 211 locations across multiple dining brands. The company serves an estimated two million meals per week and describes itself as "the future of food."

Customers and food critics alike continue to debate whether that future is desirable.

"Have you tried our new burger? I sure haven't."

— Jackie McDonall, CEO of Foodst

The company's best-known chains include BurgerFoodst, GrillFoodst, PancakeFoodst, LateNightFoodst, and AirportFoodst, the latter being famous for charging customers premium prices for disappointment.

Foodst is renowned for its experimental menu development process. Many products contain proprietary ingredient blends whose exact composition remains a closely guarded trade secret.

A 2018 marketing campaign promoted a burger containing meat from seven separate animal species. The campaign won several awards and triggered an investigation from multiple government agencies.

Despite regular controversy, Foodst remains one of the country's most trusted restaurant brands, largely because most alternatives are owned by Foodst as well.

### Jupiter

Jupiter is Etheria's largest telecommunications and information technology company. It provides mobile networks, internet services, cloud hosting, cybersecurity solutions, data centres, and technical support that may eventually answer the phone.

Founded as a regional telephone operator, Jupiter expanded aggressively during the digital era and now controls a significant portion of Etheria's communications infrastructure.

"We connect people with each other and charge money for it. That's probably the most human thing ever."

During the Great Network Failure of 2017, nearly all government websites became inaccessible for six hours. Jupiter described the incident as a "temporary cloud-based networking event."

Customer satisfaction surveys consistently rank Jupiter among Etheria's companies.

### Manen

"You probably own something made by us. And if you don't, we'll probably acquire the company that made it."

— Mike Manen, CEO of Manen

"A Ferro should last a lifetime. Whether that's the car's lifetime or yours depends on maintenance."

Critics have occasionally accused the company of prioritizing quantity over quality. Ferro maintains that reliability should be measured by whether the vehicle eventually reaches its destination.

The Ferro Falcon was once voted "Most Likely Car To Still Exist In Twenty Years," an award the company proudly displays in its headquarters.

## Transport

### Railway

The history of rail transport in Etheria began in 1839 with the opening of the Coax City-Turing Transportation Foundation (CCTF) line between Coax City and Turing. Originally established as a non-profit organization, the line proved so successful that it was extended to Barbara in the south and Apfel in the north just two years later.

The early railway industry quickly became crowded. Interrail Maxwell Corporation (I-Max) was founded in 1842 to connect Maxwell and Bling, while the Yaht-Bling Line (YBL) and the Bell-Tone-Ring Line (BTRL) followed shortly thereafter. By the mid-1850s, a patchwork of competing rail companies had connected virtually every major settlement in Etheria.

Unfortunately, the railways had been built with considerably more enthusiasm than planning. Several companies underestimated maintenance costs, while others purchased locomotives that struggled with mountains, deserts, corners, or, in some cases, movement in general.

The first major casualty was Nipkow North Railway (NNR), which went bankrupt in 1860. To prevent Transponder Valley from becoming isolated, the government established the National Etherian Railway Association (NERA) and constructed an alternative line between Bell and Maxwell. Passengers strongly disliked the route, noting that it was significantly slower and removed the scenic mountain journey entirely. The government eventually reopened the original line at a higher ticket price, proving that people are willing to pay extra for views.

While public rail expanded, private companies struggled. In 1864, CCTF sued the government for creating "unfair competition" through NERA. The lawsuit failed spectacularly, leaving CCTF short on cash and vulnerable to a takeover by I-Max.

I-Max quickly became the dominant railway operator. Following a series of acquisitions, it introduced express services between the country's largest cities while reducing service to smaller towns. Passengers complained, shareholders celebrated, and profits surged. By 1869, I-Max controlled more than 85% of the railway market.

Concerned that a single railway company might accidentally become more powerful than the government, Parliament passed the Anti-Monopoly Act of 1869, limiting companies to 50% market share in any industry. As a result, I-Max was broken up into MaxNorth and MaxSouth in 1870.

The following decades were marked by mergers, rebrandings, and questionable business decisions. The most notable example was NipNewCo, which rebranded itself as Etherian Eagle in 1901 after a newly appointed CEO declared eagles to be "the future of transportation." The company survived for less than a year before going bankrupt.

To maintain services, the government temporarily handed the routes to NERA, which operated trains using leased rolling stock and considerable optimism. A permanent successor, Etherian Eastern, was established in 1903.

The railway industry spent much of the twentieth century changing names while remaining broadly the same. MaxNorth became Skyponder Railways, briefly renamed itself NORTHERN, then changed back again when customers failed to notice the difference. MaxSouth adopted the more respectable-sounding Southern Etherian Railway Company (SERC).

By the 1950s, many passengers suspected that the rail companies had stopped competing altogether. The companies shared trains, engineers, schedules, and sometimes management. Critics described the arrangement as "three monopolies wearing a trench coat."

Investigations during the 1960s revealed decades of corruption, favoritism, and financial misconduct across both the railway companies and NERA itself. Public confidence collapsed. Riots, strikes, and political scandals followed, ultimately resulting in a landslide victory for a socialist government.

In 1972, NERA was reorganized into the Etherian Transportation Foundation (ETF), which was tasked with nationalizing the country's buses and railways. The transition proved chaotic. Staff shortages, uncertainty, and mass resignations turned rail travel into an adventure few passengers had voluntarily selected.

By 1977, ETF operated all rail services in Etheria. To prevent future corruption, infrastructure maintenance was assigned to a separate organization, the Etherian Transport Maintenance Foundation (ETMF).

The last remnants of the private operators survived through bus services and station retail concessions. Former rivals Skyponder and SERC ultimately merged in 1979 to form TransEtheria, ending over a century of railway competition and beginning a new era of arguing about bus timetables instead.

### Maxwell International Harbor
Maxwell International Harbor is the largest seaport in Etheria and one of the busiest ports in the Union. Located on the western coast near the city of Maxwell, the harbor serves as the country's primary gateway for international trade and handles millions of tons of cargo each year.

Construction of the harbor began in 1818 when local merchants concluded that loading goods onto ships was significantly more efficient than carrying them across the country by horse. The first docks were completed in 1822 and quickly attracted traders from across the Union. Over the following century, the harbor expanded repeatedly, often in response to ships becoming larger than engineers had anticipated.

Today, the harbor consists of dozens of terminals specializing in container shipping, bulk cargo, passenger ferries, fishing operations, and various activities that customs officials prefer not to discuss publicly.

"If it exists somewhere in the world, there's a good chance it passed through Maxwell at least once."

— Marina Dockwell, Harbor Authority Director

The harbor is closely connected to Etheria's rail and road networks, making it a crucial economic hub. Historians often credit the growth of Maxwell from a modest coastal town into a major city almost entirely to the success of its port.

Throughout its history, Maxwell Harbor has faced fierce competition from ports elsewhere in the Union. Its traditional response has been to build larger docks, taller cranes, and increasingly confident advertising campaigns.

A long-standing local joke claims that every citizen of Maxwell either works in the harbor, knows someone who works in the harbor, or is currently waiting for something stuck in the harbor.

The harbor's most famous landmark is the Titan Crane, a massive loading crane completed in 1938. While officially intended for cargo operations, it has become one of Etheria's most photographed structures and appears on more postcards than several actual tourist attractions.

### Coax International Aiport
Coax International Airport (CIA) is the largest airport in Etheria and serves as the country's primary gateway to the rest of the Union and beyond. Located just outside Coax City, the airport handles the majority of Etheria's international passenger traffic and is the main hub for the national airline, EtherAir.

The airport opened in 1937 after government officials concluded that trains, ships, and optimism alone could not support the nation's growing economy. Originally consisting of a single runway, a control tower, and a building described by contemporary newspapers as "adequate," Coax International steadily expanded throughout the twentieth century.

"When people think of Etheria, we want Coax International to be their first impression. Preferably before they start complaining about baggage claim."

— Amelia Runway, Airport Director

Today, the airport offers connections to destinations throughout the Union and maintains direct services to several intercontinental destinations. Its most famous feature is the Grand Terminal Hall, a massive glass structure completed in 1968 that won multiple architectural awards and nearly as many maintenance complaints.

Coax International has repeatedly been voted Etheria's best airport by business travelers, largely because it is the only airport where one can realistically make a connection without sprinting.

The airport's unofficial motto, coined by passengers rather than management, is:

"At least you're already in the right city."

### Newcast International Aiport
Newcast International Airport (NIA) is Etheria's second-largest airport and the country's leading hub for low-cost carriers, holiday flights, and questionable life choices booked at 2 a.m.

Located outside Newcast, the airport was originally established in 1958 as a military airfield before transitioning to civilian use several years later. During the rise of commercial aviation, it became the preferred airport for budget-conscious travelers and airlines seeking lower operating costs.

"Why pay more to arrive at the same destination?"

— Barry Bargain, CEO of Newcast Airport Holdings

The airport is known for offering some of the cheapest flights in the Union. Critics have noted that these low prices occasionally come at the expense of comfort, convenience, and arriving in the city advertised on the ticket.

By the late 1960s, Newcast had earned the nickname "The People's Airport", though competing airports preferred the term "The Discount Runway."

Its terminal buildings prioritize efficiency over luxury. Travelers can typically move from the entrance to their departure gate in under ten minutes, assuming they can successfully navigate the maze of duty-free advertisements.

The airport's most celebrated achievement came in 1967 when it won the Union Aviation Award for "Highest Passenger Growth." The following year it received the less prestigious distinction of "Most Lost Luggage Per Square Meter."

Despite its reputation, Newcast remains enormously popular. Millions of travelers choose the airport every year, proving that people will tolerate a surprising amount of inconvenience in exchange for a cheaper ticket.

### EPB: Etherian Public Broadcast
The Etherian Public Broadcasting Foundation (EPB) is the national public broadcaster of Etheria. Established by the government in 1925, it began as the country's first radio service and remains one of its most influential media institutions.

The organization was created during a period of economic instability following one of the most severe market crashes in Etherian history. Government officials viewed radio as a powerful new technology capable of informing the public, promoting national unity, and, perhaps most importantly, preventing further panic.

Commercial radio broadcasting was prohibited from the outset. Policymakers argued that the airwaves were too important to be left to private interests or people with "unverified opinions."

Initially, broadcasts originated from the capital city of Turing and aired for only two hours per day. Programming consisted primarily of government announcements, news bulletins, weather reports, and occasional classical music performances whenever something newsworthy failed to happen.

Despite never having an official name, listeners commonly referred to the station as Turing Radio Set. Broadcasters gradually adopted the name themselves, and by 1927 it had become the station's unofficial identity.

As radio ownership increased across the country, it became apparent that signals from Turing struggled to cross the Amplify Hills and reach the rapidly growing communities of Transponder Valley. In response, the government established a small relay station in the city of Bell, operating under the name Bell Radio.

The new station was initially intended to produce its own local programming. However, operating two separate radio services proved expensive, and Bell Radio soon transitioned to rebroadcasting recorded content from Turing. To reflect its increasingly national role, the organization abandoned local branding and adopted the name Etherian Public Broadcasting.

In 1935 the broadcaster launched a second national radio network. The original service was renamed Radio 1, while the new station became Radio 2.

Radio 1 focused primarily on news, current affairs, sports coverage, and educational programming. Radio 2 specialized in music and light entertainment. Historians generally regard this division as the moment Etherian citizens first gained the ability to complain about "what's on the other channel."

Throughout the 1940s, EPB expanded its network of transmitters and became one of the country's most trusted sources of information. During national emergencies, broadcasts were often carried simultaneously on both stations. During less urgent periods, listeners could enjoy uninterrupted debates about agricultural policy.

In 1951, EPB conducted Etheria's first experimental television broadcasts. The new service was launched under the name EPB-TV, a title that remains in use today.

Television ownership remained limited during the early years, and many Etherians first encountered the technology through public demonstrations in town squares, government buildings, and shop windows. Contemporary reports suggest that a significant portion of viewers spent their first broadcasts wondering why radio suddenly required furniture.

Following the success of the television service, the broadcaster standardized its branding. The "EPB" name was officially adopted across all radio and television operations, creating the foundation of the organization still recognized today.

By 1970, after decades of public broadcasting dominance, the government legalized commercial broadcasting. For the first time, EPB faced competition from privately owned radio and television companies.

The decision transformed Etheria's media landscape. While supporters welcomed increased choice and competition, critics feared a future in which broadcasters might prioritize ratings over public service.

EPB's management publicly dismissed such concerns, insisting that citizens would always prefer educational programming, balanced journalism, and three-hour documentaries about rural irrigation infrastructure.

Events would soon prove otherwise.
