# Voice-over script

Everything Uncle Tabby and Penny Cash say in the tutorial, in the order a player hears it. This file is written by `npm run voice-over` from `src/config/guide.ts`; change a line there, not here.

## How to use it

- **One recording per line**, named after the line: `tabby-welcome.mp3`, `studio.mp3` and so on. The file name is under each title. They go in `public/voice/`.
- **The text on screen is the text below, word for word.** If you change a word while recording, tell me and I change the line to match.
- **Only the quoted text is spoken.** The numbered how-to list a step shows under it is read, not heard.
- **Nothing names the player or the company**, and no line has a figure that could change with the game’s balance, so a recording stays right.
- **70 lines in all**: 4 for Uncle Tabby (127 words, about 1 min) and 66 for Penny Cash (2464 words, about 18 min).

## To record

15 lines wait for a recording. Until a file is in, the line is shown and not heard: `look-around.mp3` (new), `ceo-office.mp3` (new), `reception.mp3` (new), `mcr.mp3` (new), `technician-hire.mp3` (new), `clock.mp3` (new), `titles-low.mp3` (new), `stale-titles.mp3` (new), `master-control.mp3` (new), `audiences.mp3` (new), `writers.mp3` (new), `cook.mp3` (new), `strike.mp3` (new), `notice.mp3` (new), `top-floor.mp3` (new). Each is marked below.

## The two voices

- **Uncle Tabby** (Tabby Loid, newspaper publisher, your uncle and your first financier). A newspaper man who has just won an argument that took him years: warm, loud, in a hurry, fond of you and fonder of his money.
- **Penny Cash** (Your uncle’s business consultant, on loan to you). Dry and quick. She states things once and trusts you to keep up. The jokes are told with a straight face; she is on your side, and it shows most when things go wrong.

## Uncle Tabby: the welcome

### 1. Welcome

File: `tabby-welcome.mp3`

> There you are! Come in, come in. All my life I have printed the news of Etheria. Today, the news is you.

### 2. The new law

File: `tabby-law.mp3`

> The government has finally given in. From this morning, anyone may make radio and television, not just the public broadcaster. I lobbied for that law for years. Somebody in the family ought to use it.

### 3. The deal

File: `tabby-deal.mp3`

> So here is the deal. I put up the money to start. You build the company. And once a year I take my share of whatever you have not put back into it. Fair is fair.

### 4. One more thing

File: `tabby-penny.mp3`

> One more thing. I am lending you Penny Cash, my business consultant. She is sharp, she is honest, and she will tell you so herself. Good luck. Make me proud. And make me rich.

### 18. The schedule

### 20. A technician

### 21. On the air

File: `play.mp3`

### 22. Day and night

File: `clock.mp3`  
**To record: a new line.**

> One thing about the clock, before you wonder. A month in the game is one working day in the office: the clock goes from eight in the morning to ten at night as the month goes by. ### 23. The first month

File: `first-month.mp3`

It will show a lot of money gone: we bought furniture and a license, and those you pay for only once. Look at the running result instead. That says whether the company pays its own way.

### 24. Into the black

File: `black.mp3`

> There is January, and the company does not pay its own way yet. Do not panic. Listeners take a while to find a new station. Let February run, and we look again.

### 25. Someone in sales

File: `sales.mp3`

> A month in the black! Your uncle will have it framed. And it opens a door: sponsors only talk to a company that pays its own way. Advertisers already pay you for every listener. A sponsor pays more, for one show. Hire someone in sales, and give them a desk.

### 26. Your first sponsor

File: `sponsor.mp3`

> Now we wait for Monday: that is when the offers come in. Read the small print before you sign. The show has to keep its listeners above the minimum, or you pay a penalty. Give it to a show that clears the bar with room to spare.

### 27. Someone in marketing

File: `marketing.mp3`

> A sponsor on the books. Now for the listeners you do not have yet: nobody tunes in to a channel they have never heard of. A marketer runs a campaign that makes one channel, or one show, more appealing to the audience you choose. Hire one, and find them a desk too.

### 28. A first campaign

File: `campaign.mp3`

> Start small. Promote the channel to everyone, on the smallest budget, for a few weeks, and watch what the ratings do. A campaign costs money every week it runs, so never start one you cannot pay for.

### 29. On your own

File: `finish.mp3`

> That is the lot, for now. You have a company that pays its way, a sponsor, and people who are starting to know your name. From here it is yours. I will only drop by when something new comes up. And if you would rather not see me at all, Settings is where you send me away.

## Penny Cash: hints

Each is said once, the first time it matters. A game does not have to reach all of them. 28 lines, 1081 words, about 8 min.

### 1. In the red

File: `red.mp3`  
Said: The first time the money is below zero. This one can interrupt the first weeks.

> We need to talk. The company is in the red, and the bank will not watch that for long. Cut what does not earn: a host without a show, a room nobody uses. A loan buys you time, if the bank will lend. And fill every slot on your schedule.

### 2. Your uncle steps in

File: `bailout.mp3`  
Said: When the bank would have closed the company down and Uncle Tabby has paid in, which he does once. This one can interrupt the first weeks too.

> That was close. The bank was about to close the doors, and your uncle has paid them off. He asked me to tell you that it was the first time and the last. So use the months it buys you: cut what does not earn, fill every slot, and get back above zero.

### 3. A stale show

File: `stale.mp3`  
Said: The first time a show's freshness drops below 80%.

> One of your shows is going stale. Listeners notice long before you do. A revamp makes it fresh again. It costs a little, and it is worth it.

### 4. Running out of titles

File: `titles-low.mp3`  
Said: The first time the bought titles of a programme on the schedule last a week or less.  
**To record: a new line.**

> Your library is running low. When the last episode has aired, that slot shows reruns, and the viewers go elsewhere. Buy more on the content market before it comes to that.

### 5. Tired titles

File: `stale-titles.mp3`  
Said: The first time a bought title airs at less than half its freshness.  
**To record: a new line.**

> Your viewers have seen these titles too often. A title needs rest between airings. Buy more of them, so that each one airs less often and comes back fresh.

### 6. A second region

File: `second-region.mp3`  
Said: After a month in the black, once the money covers the cheapest license the channel doesn't have, with some to spare.

> You can afford a license in a second region. The same shows, the same people, and more listeners: growth does not come cheaper than that.

### 7. A second station

File: `talk-station.mp3`  
Said: After a month in the black, while the company has one channel, once the money covers a radio talk studio, a second manager and a second license.

> Here is a thought. Your station plays music, and music is for the young. The people with money to spend, the professionals and the older listeners, want to be talked to: news, call-ins, sport. That takes a second station. A radio talk studio, a channel with a manager of its own, a license, and hosts who can hold a conversation.

### 8. Master control

File: `master-control.mp3`  
Said: The first time a channel has a license while the company has no master control room, or nobody to work it.  
**To record: a new line.**

> Your channel is off the air before it has begun. Everything you broadcast goes out through a master control room: a long desk and a wall of monitors, and a technician to sit at it. Build one, and your channel reaches the antenna.

### 9. Who is listening

File: `audiences.mp3`  
Said: Once the first weeks are over and the company has had four monthly reports and a month in the black.  
**To record: a new line.**

> A word about your schedule. Every slot has its own crowd: workers and families in the morning, students and teens late at night. Give each crowd what it likes, and keep three slots of one kind to stand out. And when you grow, do not make a second station that plays the same thing. Aim it at the people your first one leaves to the others.

### 10. Worn equipment

File: `technician.mp3`  
Said: The first time a piece of equipment is worn below 70% and there is no technician.

> Your equipment is wearing out. A broken mixing desk takes half the studio with it. Hire a technician before it gets that far.

### 11. A fault in the building

File: `maintenance.mp3`  
Said: The first time the lighting or the heating fails in a room and there are no maintenance staff.

> Something in the building has failed, and nobody in that room is happy about it. Maintenance staff repair the heating and the lighting. You do not have any yet.

### 12. Writers, not a host

File: `writers.mp3`  
Said: The first time the company has a sitcom, soap opera or reality studio, or such a show, and no writers.  
**To record: a new line.**

> A sitcom has no host. A cast plays it, and the writers make it funny. Build a writers’ room with a story board and a place for each of them, then hire a few. Soaps and reality TV work the same way, and a kids’ show or a radio drama gets better with writers too.

### 13. A canteen

File: `canteen.mp3`  
Said: With ten people on the payroll, no canteen, and money for one.

> Your team has grown, and it eats at its desks. Give them a canteen. People with good morale do better work, and better work is better ratings. Build a room, make it a canteen and buy what its checklist asks for.

### 14. Hot meals

File: `cook.mp3`  
Said: With a canteen that has all it needs, fifteen people on the payroll, no cook, and money to spare.  
**To record: a new line.**

> A canteen keeps your people fed, but a sandwich and a coffee only go so far. Put a stove in it and hire a cook. A hot lunch does more for morale than you would think.

### 15. Trouble on the floor

File: `strike.mp3`  
Said: The first time a team threatens to strike.  
**To record: a new line.**

> Some of your people have had enough, and they say they will walk out. Find out what is wrong: a dirty office, a broken heater, nowhere to eat. A meeting with their manager helps for a month. And if they do walk out, a bonus brings them back, at a price.

### 16. Research

File: `research.mp3`  
Said: After four monthly reports, while nobody works in research and development.

> A word about the future. Your rivals get new technology in the year it is invented, and a studio that falls behind makes weaker shows. Hire someone for research and development, and you can even be a few years early.

### 17. Television

File: `television.mp3`  
Said: After half a year, with the last three months in the black, once the money covers the cheapest TV license that is for sale, a library and a few months of series, and there is no TV channel yet. It can also be started from the Settings.

> You can afford television now. It does not have to start big. No studio, no cameras, no presenters: a channel, a license, and series that you buy by the episode. Shall I walk you through it?

### 18. A license auction

File: `auction.mp3`  
Said: The first time a new license is auctioned for a medium the company broadcasts in.

> A license is being auctioned. Once the free licenses in a region are gone, a new one goes to the highest bidder. Bid what it is worth to you before the closing day, and not a coin more.

### 19. A renewal round

File: `renewal.mp3`  
Said: The first time one of the company's own licenses goes into a renewal round.

> One of your licenses is up for renewal. A license does not last forever, and when its term ends, your rivals may bid for it. As the holder you have the advantage. It is not a free pass. Bid, or lose the region.

### 20. A rival wants your host

File: `poaching.mp3`  
Said: The first time a rival makes one of the company's hosts an offer.

> A rival has made one of your hosts an offer. Fame makes people expensive. Match it, or let them walk and watch the listeners follow.

### 21. A month’s notice

File: `notice.mp3`  
Said: The first time one of the company’s hosts has signed with a rival and works out their notice.  
**To record: a new line.**

> One of your hosts is leaving us, but not today: they work out the month first. That month is yours. Hire a new host and give them the show before the last day, and the audience hardly notices.

### 22. The country is watching

File: `event.mp3`  
Said: The first time a World Cup, the Olympics, an election campaign or a recession is on.

> Big events change what people want to hear. During a World Cup or the Olympics, sports draws a crowd. In an election campaign, the news does. And in a recession, advertisers pay less for everything. The news ticker tells you what is coming.

### 23. Event rights

File: `rights.mp3`  
Said: The first time the TV rights to a sports event are auctioned while the company has a TV channel.

> The television rights to a big sports event are being auctioned. On television, only the broadcaster that holds them gets the crowd. If you have a sports show, it may be worth a bid.

### 24. An event of your own

File: `own-event.mp3`  
Said: Once the money above the reserve covers a concert.

> You have money to spare. Stage a concert. It lifts your reputation, it makes your best host famous, and it earns prestige, which is the other score your uncle brags about.

### 25. Your uncle’s share

File: `dividend.mp3`  
Said: The first December in which the owners will take a dividend.

> December. On the first of January your uncle takes his share: part of the cash you hold above a year of running costs. Whatever you put back into the company before then stays in the company.

### 26. A second floor

File: `second-floor.mp3`  
Said: When the company is worth enough to build on the second floor.

> The company is worth enough to build on the next floor. More rooms: more studios, more desks, and somewhere to put that canteen.

### 27. The top floor

File: `top-floor.mp3`  
Said: When the company is worth enough to build on the top floor of its building, from the third floor up.  
**To record: a new line.**

> The top floor is yours. There is no floor above it, so plan it well: a studio takes more room than you think, and the writers want a room of their own.

### 28. Who sits where

File: `places.mp3`  
Said: When the company has a second office.

> A second office. Until now your people sat wherever a desk was free. You can give them a place of their own: a whole department in one room, with its manager next door. The panel of an office shows who sits where.

## Penny Cash: television

One line per step, for a player who says yes when her hint “Television” offers to walk them through it. 7 lines, 236 words, about 2 min.

### 1. A TV channel

File: `tv-channel.mp3`

> First the channel itself. Start a second channel, and make it television. It needs a name of its own.

### 2. A manager of its own

File: `tv-manager.mp3`

> A channel manager runs one channel, and yours has a radio station to mind. Hire a second one. Give them a desk in the management office, and put them in charge of television.

### 3. A television license

File: `tv-license.mp3`

> Now the permit. Television licenses are scarce: the authority hands out only a few, and every one of them costs a small fortune. Buy one where one is for sale. One region is enough to begin with.

### 4. A library

File: `tv-library.mp3`

> No studio. Not yet. Television can start with programmes that somebody else made, and those need a home. Build a library, and put shelves in it.

### 5. Series

File: `tv-titles.mp3`

> Now the content market. Buy series. They come by the episode, one for every evening, so buy enough for a few weeks. Leave the films for later: a film costs twice as much, for one night.

### 6. On the air

File: `tv-show.mp3`

> Make a programme of them: a new show, from the library, of the genre series. Then put it in the evening. That is when the whole country sits down to watch.

### 7. Television, the small way

File: `tv-finish.mp3`

> And that is a television channel. One programme every evening, and reruns around it. Keep the library stocked: when the episodes run out, so does the audience. Once it earns its keep, add documentaries in the afternoon. They are cheap. A studio and shows of your own can wait until the money is there.
