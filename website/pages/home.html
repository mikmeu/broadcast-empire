One radio station, 1970

For years your uncle, the newspaper publisher Tabby Loid, lobbied the government of Etheria to let companies make radio and television. On 1 January 1970 the law changes. He puts up the money and lends you his business consultant, Penny Cash. The company is yours.

Broadcast Empire is a tycoon game. You rent a floor, build a studio, hire hosts and put a first show on the air. From there you grow, year by year, into television and across the five regions of the country, against three commercial rivals and the public broadcaster.

The game is free, and it is an alpha: it changes with every version, and what players ask for decides what comes next.

What you do

Build the office

Draw rooms on the floor, place the furniture by hand and give people a desk of their own. The office is in 3D, with the town, the traffic and the railway around it, and everyone in it keeps office hours.

Make the programs

Hire hosts and producers, make shows and fill the day from the morning to late at night, for the people who are tuned in at that hour. Radio comes first. Television follows: bought series at the start, then studios for news, talk shows, game shows and soaps.

Run the company

Win sponsors and keep them, run campaigns, bid for licenses in every region and research what comes next, from FM stereo to streaming. The monthly report says whether it pays.

In the game today

  • A company from 1970 onward, in five regions with audiences of their own
  • Rooms you draw, furniture and studio equipment you place, up to six floors
  • A master control room every channel goes out through, control rooms for the TV studios, and the cables up to the antenna
  • An inbox where every piece of news is an e-mail from the person it concerns, windows you can resize, and a staffing report that says whether a show has enough people
  • Radio and television, live shows and bought content, and a streaming service of your own for the last twenty years
  • The sounds of the office and the town: typewriters, phones, the canteen, gulls and the train going by
  • Writers, video editors and a studio for every kind of show, with a cast on the set
  • Sponsors you get to know, marketing campaigns, license auctions
  • A research tree by decade
  • The law at your door: lawsuits against bold shows, lawyers on your staff, and a broadcasting authority that fines a channel for breaking its rules
  • Sports events, elections with presidents of their own, radio and TV awards and recessions
  • A living office: dirt, wear, a canteen with a cook, team meetings, the post, and strikes when morale stays low
  • A tutorial with the voices of Uncle Tabby and Penny Cash
  • Milestones that earn your company levels, which open the game step by step, and bring money from Uncle Tabby, and a wiki inside it
  • Named saves, three difficulties that set the money and the rivals, colors that turn dark when night falls in the game
